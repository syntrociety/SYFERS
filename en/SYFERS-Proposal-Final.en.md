# ORRI INCUBATOR GRANTS - PROPOSAL

## SYFERS - Syntropic Framework for Encoding Regenerative Societies

---

## 1. OBJECTIVES, NEEDS and APPROACH [Excellence]

### 1.1 Objectives

#### Overall aim

To turn Sulitânia, an already active rural cooperative and Living Lab in Southern Portugal, where community members and volunteers work daily on regeneration, into a reference site for ORRI-driven, syntropic and regenerative innovation, and to develop a transferable ORRI Tool that enables other rural regions to follow the same path.

#### Specific objectives

1. Design and test a Syntropic Living Lab (SLL) governance model for Sulitânia Cooperative that applies ORRI principles (public engagement, gender & minority inclusion, open science, ethics, science education and good governance) to real territorial challenges.

2. Co-create and implement integrated pilots across three pillars:
   - ecological regeneration (soil, water, biodiversity, food production);
   - social & relational ecosystem (community governance, inclusion, wellbeing);
   - economic / learning activities (training, micro-enterprises, regenerative tourism)
   showing how they reinforce each other in one rural site.

3. Develop one ORRI Tool – the Syntropic Framework for Encoding Regenerative Societies (SYFERS) that codifies methods, templates and lessons from Sulitânia into a practical guide for other rural organisations and municipalities.

4. Strengthen institutional capacity for ORRI and RRI within Sulitânia Cooperative embedding gender & minority inclusion, open science and participatory decision-making into their permanent practices and strategies.

These objectives are realistic within a 12-month Incubator project and measurable through:

- number and quality of pilots implemented;
- adoption of the governance model and ORRI practices in internal rules;
- completion and external validation of the ORRI Tool;
- documented changes in skills, attitudes and procedures in both partner organisations.

### 1.2 Needs and proposed approach to address them

#### Needs

The project is based in rural Southern Portugal (Castro Marim area), a territory facing:

- **Ecological stress**: drought, soil degradation, forest fires and biodiversity loss, aggravated by climate change and historical land use.
- **Socio-economic fragility**: ageing population, seasonal tourism, limited local employment and out-migration of young people.
- **Fragmented initiatives**: environmental, social and economic projects often operate separately, with limited integration or long-term governance.
- **Implementation gap**: EU Green Deal, SDG's and national recovery plans set ambitious goals, but local actors lack concrete, human-scale models to test and adapt.

At the same time, Sulitânia and Quinta da Fornalha already host:

- a developing food forest and syntropic agroforestry systems;
- an emerging co-housing and community structure;
- regenerative tourism and small-scale production;
- a diverse group of community members with strong motivation but limited experience in ORRI methodologies.

The central need is therefore a structured, participatory and replicable way to connect these strands into one coherent, regenerative and socially inclusive "micro-infrastructure" that can be documented and shared.

#### Main potential challenges

1. **Limited experience with ORRI processes** — Local actors are motivated and with high potential but unfamiliar with systematic reflection, multi-stakeholder facilitation, and iterative co-creation.

2. **Cultural and social diversity** — Differences in languages, backgrounds, expectations and capacities may create misunderstandings or uneven participation.

3. **Risk of project overload** — High ambition in a small community can lead to fatigue or confusion about priorities without a clear structure.

4. **Integration across pillars** — Ecological, social and economic activities naturally evolve at different speeds; aligning them requires careful coordination.

5. **Documentation & scaling** — Turning living, intuitive processes into clear, transferable models is complex and requires continuous discipline.

6. **Local-to-global translation** — Creating a framework that is deeply rooted in place while remaining adaptable to other rural regions.

#### Proposed approach

Our approach is to treat Sulitânia as a Syntropic Living Lab (SLL):

- a real place where land, people and systems are interdependent;
- a quadruple helix environment, involving citizens/community members, local businesses, municipalities/authorities and knowledge actors;
- a regenerative micro-infrastructure that integrates ecological, social and economic flows.

Key features of the approach:

- **Co-creation from within**: community members, workers, volunteers, guests and local partners are involved in diagnosing needs, designing pilots and reflecting on results.
- **Syntropic leadership**: leadership is understood as creating conditions for life to organise itself, rather than controlling people. This means focusing on coherence, clear roles, transparent decision-making and responsibility.
- **ORRI as backbone**: we explicitly use ORRI principles to structure reflection, design and evaluation. The SYFERS will be built around the ORRI approach.
- **"Glocal" perspective**: we address local needs in Castro Marim while designing tools, indicators and formats that make it easy for other rural regions to adapt the model.
- **Iterative learning**: pilots are designed in short cycles: observe → prototype → evaluate → adjust, with continuous documentation and open sharing. This prevents project overload.

### 1.2.1 Inclusion, Gender Equality and Minorities

In syntropic systems, diversity is a source of order, resilience and intelligence. Therefore, for Sulitânia, inclusion is a core leadership principle, not an administrative requirement.

Our communities already include people of different genders, ages, cultures, abilities and socio-economic backgrounds. In this project we will:

- **Treat inclusion as a syntropic principle**: ensuring that minority voices (non-binary, LGBTQIA+, migrants, neurodiverse, low-income, elders, youth) are seen, heard and integrated in all decision-making.
- **Ensure balanced participation**: avoiding domination by any single gender or group, and ensuring diverse representation across facilitation, meetings and ORRI events.
- **Respect gender identity and expression**: including the use of chosen pronouns and inclusive communication in English and Portuguese.
- **Make power dynamics discussable**: creating facilitated spaces to address hidden biases related to gender, age, culture or background.
- **Use restorative conflict processes**: focusing on awareness and repair, while ensuring safety and support for anyone who feels marginalised.

In short: inclusion, equality and diversity are living practices that allow syntropy to emerge.

### 1.2.2 Open Science

We will integrate Open Science practices by:

- Documenting methods, processes and tools openly (in English and Portuguese) and sharing them through a dedicated website and/or open repository (e.g. Zenodo or OSF);
- Publishing the SYFERS as an open ORRI Tool under a Creative Commons licence, so other regions can adapt it;
- Collecting and sharing non-sensitive data (e.g. soil indicators, biodiversity observations, participation metrics, workshop formats) in reusable formats;
- Involving citizens as co-researchers, particularly in monitoring ecological indicators, mapping social networks and reflecting on governance;
- Participating in ORRI and REINFORCING knowledge-sharing activities, contributing our results to the wider community of practice.

## 2. CHANGES TO BE ACHIEVED [Impact]

Our project aims for three levels of change:

### 2.1 Organisational and ORRI institutional change

For Sulitânia Cooperative:

- A tested SLL governance model integrating ORRI principles into daily practice (meeting formats, decision-making, conflict resolution, role definition).
- Established internal procedures for gender & minority aware, inclusive and participatory processes.
- Strengthened Open Science culture, with regular documentation, transparent sharing and citizen involvement.
- Improved skills and confidence of staff and community members in facilitation, co-creation, monitoring and evaluation.

These changes will be formalised in updated internal guidelines and governance documents, and monitored through self-assessment at start, mid-term and end of the project.

### 2.2 Territorial impact (Castro Marim / Southern Portugal)

- A functioning regenerative micro-infrastructure in Sulitânia:
  - improved soil and water management;
  - more diverse and productive food systems;
  - better organised shared spaces and community structures;
  - pilots for regenerative micro-businesses (e.g. food forest products, educational programmes, ecotourism activities).
- Increased engagement of local actors (citizens, SME's, schools, municipality) in ORRI-style co-creation.
- Concrete contributions to SDG's – especially SDG 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16 and 17 – by showing how they can be addressed simultaneously in one place.
- A visible example of a rural region moving from vulnerability to regeneration, providing hope and practical reference for similar territories in Southern Portugal and beyond.

### 2.3 Wider societal and knowledge impact

- SYFERS – a structured guide and toolkit that combines:
  - ORRI principles and tools;
  - syntropic and regenerative design concepts;
  - practical templates and case examples from Sulitânia;
  - guidance on gender & minority inclusion, open science and quadruple helix engagement.
- Transferable learning for other rural communities, cooperatives, municipalities and SME's who wish to implement ORRI-based living labs.
- Contribution to European discussions on how to align Green Deal, SDG's and rural development with real community-led practice.

### 3.2 Description of the consortium

#### Sulitânia Cooperative (Coordinator)

Sulitânia is a rural cooperative in Southern Portugal where ecological restoration, community life and new forms of local economy grow side by side. We are rooted on the land of Quinta da Fornalha, an ecological farm that has been regenerating soil and biodiversity for more than twenty years. It is a place where ancient history lines meet and where people have lived in relationship with nature for a long time. This depth in the land gives our work a clarity and direction that cannot be manufactured.

What began as a group of people choosing to live in deeper alignment with the land and with each other has become a living laboratory for regenerative practices. We work without ideology and without fixed models. Instead, we observe what the land, the people and the system need, and we develop solutions from within. This creates natural integration between ecological work, social cooperation and emerging livelihoods.

Sulitânia offers a rare combination of conditions: a motivated and diverse community, active regenerative agriculture, early-stage co-housing structures, and real socio-economic challenges typical for many rural regions. This makes it an ideal place to test and refine approaches that can later be adapted elsewhere.

Our work is grounded, practical and human. We restore soil, strengthen relationships, experiment with new governance, and develop micro-economies that support both people and place. The ORRI framework allows us to document, structure and share these insights in a way that is accessible to other regions.

Sulitânia is small, but what grows here is relevant far beyond this land: a coherent, real-world example of how regeneration becomes possible when people, nature and local systems evolve together.

Sulitânia will coordinate the project, lead governance and ORRI integration, host most co-creation processes and be the main beneficiary of institutional change. The cooperative currently has limited formal experience with ORRI, which makes it an ideal Incubator beneficiary.

#### Quinta da Fornalha (Partner)

Quinta da Fornalha is an ecotourism and organic farm that provides the physical land where Sulitânia is situated. It brings decades of experience in organic agriculture, nature-based tourism and collaboration with the University of Algarve and other partners on ecological projects (including a food forest initiative).

Quinta da Fornalha will support ecological pilots (Action 2), host visitors and support communication with local institutions and networks.

#### Complementarity

- Sulitânia contributes social innovation, governance experimentation and community engagement.
- Quinta da Fornalha contributes land, ecological expertise and operational infrastructure.

Together they represent a quadruple helix nucleus:

- citizens and community members,
- an SME in sustainable tourism / agriculture,
- connections to knowledge actors (e.g. University of Algarve) and public authorities,
- and an emerging governance model that can integrate them.

This collaboration creates a realistic yet ambitious environment for testing ORRI approaches in a rural territory.

### Budget, Tables and Details

The project mobilises 5,480 productive hours (70 person-months) delivered by 6–8 part-time community members at an average cost of just 7.85 €/hour from the ORRI grant. The heaviest workload (2,100 h) falls on Action 2 and is entirely self-funded by Sulitânia (21,000 € own resources), demonstrating exceptional commitment. All roles remain part-time, ensuring sustainability and full alignment with the living-lab reality while delivering a high-quality, multilingual ORRI Tool and the physical regeneration of 2000m².

The mandatory ORRI Tool 'SYFERS – Syntropic Framework for Encoding Regenerative Societies' will be published in November 2026 on the European Commission's Zenodo repository under a CC-BY-SA 4.0 licence, receiving a permanent DOI (e.g. 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXXX) that guarantees long-term open access, citability and reusability across Europe.

Risks have been systematically identified and mitigated for each Action. The two highest risks (drought affecting planting and possible delays in the SYFERS ORRI Tool) are countered respectively by full self-funding of the ecological pilots (21,000 € own resources) and by dedicating 1,200 hours and three senior/mid-level roles exclusively to Tool development. All remaining risks are medium or low probability and have clear, low-cost contingency measures, ensuring smooth delivery of the mandatory ORRI Tool and the 2-hectare regeneration within the 12-month timeline.

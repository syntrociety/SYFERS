# Quarterly Assembly Reflections — Q1 2026

*Generated from the assembly minutes of 2026-05-09. Names are
redacted; roles are substituted. The cooperative reviewed and

---

## Context

This is the second quarter in which the cooperative has held its
reflection-by-design meeting at the end of the working period rather
than at the start of the next. The change was made deliberately at
the end of 2025 — assemblies that opened with a forward-looking
agenda were producing thinner reflections than assemblies that
opened with a backward look. The Q1 reflection happened on
2026-05-09. Thirteen members were present, two participated by
voice, one was absent with notice.

The reflection is structured around four questions: **what broke,
what held, what we learned, what is still open**. The pattern
is described in `01-governance/principles.md` and the meeting
template in `01-governance/templates/`.

What follows is a synthesis of the four sections. Specifics that
would identify members are removed; ranges are given instead of
exact figures where the exact figure would be identifying.

---

## What broke

Two things broke in Q1, and both will shape Q2.

The first is financial. The income mix that carried the cooperative
through 2025 — a combination of harvest sales, workshop fees, and
membership dues — narrowed in Q1 to a level where reserves were
drawn from for two months in a row. The board had foreseen this in
the Q4 2025 reflection but had not foreseen the pace. The financial
working group has been instructed to present an alternative funding
mix at the Q2 assembly.

The second is location. The cooperative received notice during Q1
that its current site context will close on 2026-09-30. This was
not a surprise — it was a possibility named in the 2024 covenant —
but the timeline narrowed faster than the cooperative had budgeted
for. The site search working group convened the day after notice
arrived. Two candidate territories are in early exploration.

Neither break is itself a crisis. Both, taken together, mean that
the cooperative is operating under conditions it has not operated
under before. That is the substantive content of the reflection,
not the breaks themselves.

---

## What held

Two things held, and the cooperative chose to note them.

Consent-based decision-making held across two consecutive assemblies
in which the agenda was unusually contested. No block was raised in
either meeting. Two paramount objections were lodged and resolved
within the same session. The pattern of resolution — a member
returning from harvest naming a tension early, a facilitator
holding silence rather than rushing to vote — is documented in the
meeting minutes of 2026-04-11 and 2026-04-25.

The second thing that held is harder to name. It might be called
the cooperative's tolerance for not-yet-resolved questions. The
Q4 2025 reflection ended with five open questions; four of them
were still open at the start of Q1; three of them remain open
now. None of them blocked the working week. The cooperative
appears to have learned, between 2024 and 2026, to carry questions
without resolving them prematurely.

---

## What we learned

The clearest learning from Q1 is a single sentence the assembly
arrived at by consent:

> **A Lab is not its location. A Lab is the practice that takes
> place in a territory.**

This sentence was not in the agenda. It emerged in the third hour
of the 2026-05-09 assembly, after the site search working group
presented its early findings. The sentence is now in the
cooperative's working language.

What follows from it, for the cooperative: the transition between
territories is not a setback. It is the first time the federation
form that has been articulated since 2024 is being tested by use.
A cooperative that can move between territories without losing its
practice is a different kind of entity than a cooperative that
cannot.

This is also the moment at which the cooperative began to need
language for relationships with actors it had not yet articulated
relationships with: territorial development practitioners,
cooperative architects, EU-policy interlocutors. The Q2 working
plan now includes outreach as a formal track rather than as an
incidental activity.

## What is still open

Three questions left open by Q1, in the order the assembly listed
them:

1. **Which four-helix instruments carry the cooperative across a
   territorial transition?** Specifically: which combinations of
   public funding, knowledge-institution partnership, enterprise
   alignment, and civil-society infrastructure exist for a
   cooperative moving between two territories under low-density
   conditions. The cooperative does not yet know the catalogue.

2. **What does the federation surface owe a Lab in transition?**
   The federation form was articulated assuming Labs operate in
   stable territories. A Lab in transition is a new case. The
   answer to this question is being deferred to a federation-level
   conversation in Q3.

3. **How are commitments to current visitors, current contacts,
   and current cooperative members held during the transition?**
   No member has objected to the transition itself. Several have
   raised questions about what their relationship to the
   cooperative looks like during the move. The membership working
   group will return to this at the Q2 assembly.
